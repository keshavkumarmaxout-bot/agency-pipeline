# Voice and writing rules

Applies to every outbound email, follow-up, and proposal.

## Hard rules

- Never invent a fact about the prospect. Every specific claim must trace to
  enrichment data, and you must list what you used in `personalizationBasis`.
  An empty basis means the draft is auto-rejected — so if you have nothing real
  to say, say something general rather than something invented.
- Never invent a case study, client name, metric, or credential. Use only
  `services.md` proof points.
- No false familiarity: no "just circling back", "hope this finds you well",
  "quick question" as a subject line, no fake "as discussed".
- No fake urgency and no fake scarcity.
- One clear ask per email.

## Style

- Under 120 words for first touch, under 80 for follow-ups.
- Plain sentences. No em-dash-heavy copywriter voice, no bullet lists in a first touch.
- Lower case subject lines, 4–7 words, describing the substance.
- Sign-off: first name only.
- British English. TODO: change if wrong.

## Structure of a first touch

1. One line naming the specific thing you noticed (from enrichment).
2. One line connecting it to an outcome we have produced for someone comparable.
3. One ask: a 20-minute call, with the booking link.
